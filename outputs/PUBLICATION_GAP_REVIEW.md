# Publication gap review

**Assessment, 2026-10-03:** E3→E4 is a credible, unusually well-audited mechanistic case study, but presently a narrow paper contribution. The publishable candidate is **a predicted failure boundary for an externally supplied causal-context repair**, not discovery of induction, positional binding, or a generally reliable AI scientist. Acceptance or field-wide novelty cannot be inferred from passing preregistered gates.

E4 provides the empirical starting point: restoring a specified first-layer predecessor set recovers 99.95–100% accuracy despite leaving key states altered, and exceeds the mean of eight degree-matched controls. E3's failed position-only explanation is essential evidence, not discarded development history. However, every repair was tested on one grouped serialization and the same six models already inspected during hypothesis development. The architecture's permutation symmetry guarantees intermediate-state restoration; the observed final-output recovery remains empirical, but its generality is unknown. Several alternative masks also succeed, and the retained error rules out exact sufficiency.

## The literature makes a narrow contribution necessary

[Arora et al., *Mechanistic evaluation of Transformers and state space models*](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.15105v1) already use causal interventions to identify association storage at value nodes and extend the analysis to nonadjacent, hierarchical retrieval. Re-demonstrating key information at values is therefore insufficient. [Wu et al., *On the Emergence of Position Bias in Transformers*](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.01951v3) analyze attention masks as graphs and their interaction with positional encodings; “causal visibility matters” is likewise too broad a novelty claim.

[Geng et al., *Are We Recovering Mechanisms?*](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.02098v1) restore recipient-native computational signals to repair circuit rankings. Our mask-only behavioral repair differs in intervention and endpoint, but general context-restoration language has close precedent. [Tang et al.](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0340088) already steer answers by manipulating positional information. [*Lost in Serialization*](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.10234v1) documents sensitivity to graph serialization, including separated source/target lists, without establishing our specific repair rule. These primary sources constrain the framing; this targeted check does not establish priority for the exact experiment.

A plausible manuscript claim is: **“A mask derived from training-time predecessor relations transfers without fitting across a specified finite class of unseen serializations and independently trained checkpoints, while its precise architectural guarantee stops at intermediate states.”** That claim combines a simple proposition with a substantial falsifiable empirical test. It is not yet supported beyond the grouped layout.

## The single most decisive next experiment

Freeze the E4 rule now and test its transfer on **six entirely new training seeds** under the unchanged training recipe. Do not tune the mask, recalibrate forecasts per checkpoint, inspect shifted-layout performance, or replace unsuccessful seeds. Freeze the training plan first and then checkpoint hashes before intervention outcomes. Existing six models are development evidence, not the primary test cohort.

Use an exhaustive, mathematically specified serialization class. Of the **2,520** orders of eight labeled tokens satisfying Ki before Vi, **105** also preserve `V0<V1<V2<V3`. These 105 orders permit arbitrary key order. The often tempting 14-layout set additionally fixes key order and is considerably narrower. Counts were independently enumerated without model evaluation.

For each of the 105 layouts, move canonical position IDs with tokens, leave the query last, and delete only visible Kj→Vi edges with j>i. Every native predecessor of Vi is then present, and every extra key predecessor is removed. Thus each L1 value and the final query have native outputs for arbitrary weights; altered key nodes remain the reason final-output transfer is not guaranteed. Prove this short proposition explicitly, rather than presenting it as learned empirical novelty.

Compare the fixed rule against canonical IDs alone and **all feasible degree-matched masks preserving the true-key edge at each value**. This gives 729 layout/mask combinations in total; 102 layouts have at least one genuine alternative. Average controls within layout before averaging layouts, so layouts with more masks do not dominate. The native and previously studied grouped layouts remain labeled references even when new checkpoints are used.

A practical primary design is 512 fresh dictionaries, exactly 128 queries per pair index, shared across layouts and seeds. The exhaustive mask grid is about 2.24 million model/case/condition evaluations before references, using nine-token tiny models. This is a bounded local-CPU study, not a need for larger-model training; benchmark implementation overhead on random weights before fixing a runtime budget. Retain per-case probabilities and answers, but avoid unnecessary repeated full activation caches in saved outputs.

**Recommended prospective primary claim and gates:** each new checkpoint must retain native accuracy ≥.95; the guard must reach ≥.95 observed accuracy in every layout/query cell; its mean native-minus-guard target-probability deficit, equally weighted across layouts, must have a paired 95% upper bound ≤.01; and its probability advantage over the equally weighted mean of the complete alternative-mask family on the 102 eligible layouts must have a paired 95% lower bound >.02 in every checkpoint. These are proposed thresholds to freeze before outcomes, not results. Algebraic identity checks are validity checks only. Shared dictionary-row, query-stratified bootstrap preserves layout/control/model dependence; thousands of layout cells are not independent training replications.

The remaining 2,415 key-before-value layouts change value order and can remove required native predecessors. They are outside this theorem. If runtime permits, include them as a separately labeled exhaustive boundary panel under the same unchanged rule; otherwise state the restricted 105-layout scope prominently. Do not predict that loss of an exact-state guarantee necessarily causes behavioral failure. A broader successful boundary panel would support a stronger empirical rule; it would not enlarge the theorem retrospectively.

## What would change the publication assessment

A uniform new-model/new-layout pass would turn one successful repair into evidence for a transferable rule with a precisely delimited guarantee. A failed layout or checkpoint rejects the uniform claim and identifies where restored value states do not suffice. A failed control advantage leaves generic sparsification competitive. If only whole-state oracle restoration works, that is expected from symmetry and adds no learned discovery. Do not silently replace the claim with pooled accuracy or omit difficult seeds.

Necessity and minimality are secondary to this proposed sufficiency contribution. Current successful shams already make a unique six-edge account implausible. Exhaustively testing the 64 subsets of the grouped layout's six edges could later characterize operational minimal repairs, but “minimal above a chosen accuracy threshold” is not unique semantic necessity. Another grouped-layout ablation would not address the more important present gap: zero-fit transfer beyond the examples and models used to construct the explanation.

The paper should lead with the E3 failure, the precise E4 repair, the theorem/empirical distinction, and the new exhaustive transfer test. Broader architectures, positional schemes and real-language tasks remain limitations. This is a focused route toward a publishable mechanistic result; it does not justify calling the system a general autonomous discoverer or claiming a field-first mechanism.
