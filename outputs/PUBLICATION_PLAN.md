# Toward a publishable causal-visibility study

Working plan, October 3, 2026. E1–E4 and all their frozen records remain intact.
Publishing a positive result in a repository is not, by itself, a completed
publishable study. The new goal is a submission-ready research manuscript and
reproducible evidence for a clearly delimited contribution. Acceptance and
field-wide priority cannot be promised.

The candidate contribution is a prospective transfer test of a fixed causal
visibility repair across a complete finite class of serializations, evaluated
on independently trained checkpoints. Prior work already establishes binding
at value positions, positional effects, and context restoration. Our study
must compare explicitly with those precedents and separate architectural
identities from learned behavioral recovery.

Required evidence and deliverables:

1. A mathematical characterization of the serialization class in which
   first-layer value states can be restored by deleting newly exposed edges.
   For four labeled pairs with each key before its value, 105 of 2,520 orders
   preserve value order; the class is broader than the 14 orders that preserve
   both key and value order. State restoration alone is not the behavioral claim.
2. A public prospective protocol, inputs, code, predictions, complete matched
   controls and checkpoints, remotely verified before confirmatory evaluation.
   Primary evidence must transfer to fresh fixed training seeds without fitting
   the repair to their shifted-layout behavior. Preserve all failures.
3. An independently checked result across the complete specified class, plus
   a separately defined boundary test. Report native performance and uncertainty,
   controls, errors, practical effect sizes and computational cost. Do not
   redefine failed thresholds after outcomes.
4. A manuscript with an explicit comparison to the closest literature, clear
   methods and figures, scope limits, counterexamples and a reproduction path.
   Independent skeptical review must assess the final claim against raw evidence.

The next protocol is still being designed; no E5 confirmation has run. Negative
transfer results remain informative and must change the manuscript's claim.
Additional experiments will address identified scientific gaps rather than
continue resampling until a preferred result passes.
