# E3 primary-source related-work and novelty audit

**Checked: 2026-10-03. Written before viewing E3 inputs or outcomes.** This is a targeted literature check, not an exhaustive systematic review. No model computations were run and no prediction files were changed. Search-result summaries and third-party discussions were used only to locate original papers; the comparisons below rely on the linked primary texts.

**Assessment:** prior work already demonstrates position-based binding, causal position interventions, and answer-directed steering through positional-information swaps. Therefore E3 must not claim to be the first demonstration that positional information controls binding or that a position intervention can favor a specified alternative answer. The narrower combination of controls in E3 could provide a useful reproducible result if its preregistered gates pass, but the search does not establish field-wide novelty of that exact combination.

## Closest direct precedent: positional swaps steer a specified alternative

Tang, Lake and Jazayeri, *Circuit explained: How does a transformer perform compositional generalization* (PLOS ONE, February 4, 2026), Fig. 12, swap positional information in a Question-Broadcast Head's value pathway while freezing the output-query pathway. Attention shifts toward the prescribed alternative token; original-task accuracy changes from 94% to 76%, with partial logit steering. A random-shuffle control lacks the systematic effect. Their encoder-decoder uses sinusoidal position embeddings. E3 instead reassigns learned input position IDs, tests the whole permutation grid and predicts alternate-answer accuracy, without freezing an internal query circuit. These are scope differences, not proof of novelty. [Primary article, intervention section and Methods](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0340088).

## Other closely related causal binding work

Feng and Steinhardt, *How do Language Models Bind Entities in Context?*, §3.2 and Appendix C, intervene on RoPE transformations to move or swap apparent entity/attribute positions. In their tested binding setting, moving positions causes little behavioral change, supporting binding-ID position independence. This is directly relevant prior methodology and a warning against universalizing E3: an input-level learned-embedding manipulation and an intervention on already-formed contextual representations can test different stages. Their finding is not contradicted merely by a positive result in six small learned-APE models. [Primary paper, position-independence experiment and intervention details](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.17191v2).

Gur-Arieh, Geva and Geiger, *Mixing Mechanisms: How Language Models Retrieve Bound Entities In-Context* (v2, May 28, 2026), distinguish positional, lexical and reflexive retrieval with counterfactual interchange interventions across nine models. They show that positional retrieval becomes less reliable in larger contexts and that mechanisms interact. E3's deterministic inverse-permutation rule is a restricted hypothesis in a four-pair task; success would not justify assuming a single binding mechanism in general language models. [Primary paper, §3 and counterfactual design](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.06182v2).

*Visual symbolic mechanisms: Emergent symbol processing in vision language models* studies content-independent spatial IDs for feature binding and causal intervention on those representations. The authors' visual IDs are internal representational variables, not simply the scalar position-ID arguments of a text model's input embedding lookup. This nevertheless gives further precedent for the broader idea of symbolic-index-mediated retrieval; terminology must distinguish these objects. [Primary paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.15871v2).

## The two initially supplied papers and task-structured coordinates

Singh et al., *Relative Positions Generalize, Absolute Positions Memorize: An Implicit-Bias Account of Length Generalization in Attention* (July 2026), analyzes fixed-offset retrieval and position pinning. Its learned-APE failure argument emphasizes coordinates outside the training range; Appendix C/Table 6 also tests interpolation, clamping and index rescaling. E3 instead permutes only trained in-range coordinates at unchanged sequence length and query location, targeting content-addressed association rather than fixed-offset selection. This is a genuine design distinction, but neither refutes their out-of-range result nor establishes a novel general principle. [Primary paper, §4.4 and inference-time fixes](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.18759v1).

Ruoss et al., *Randomized Positional Encodings Boost Length Generalization of Transformers* (2023), randomly samples position indices during training while preserving their order. The method applies to several position-encoding schemes and deliberately loosens the connection between physical index and assigned coordinate. E3 also separates these quantities, but uses nonmonotonic inference-time permutations in already-trained models and targets changed answer identity rather than length-generalization training. [Primary paper, §3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.16843).

Cho et al., *Position Coupling: Improving Length Generalization of Arithmetic Transformers Using Task Structure* (2024), assigns shared position IDs to task-related tokens, such as equal-significance digits, to encode known structure during training. Thus supplying task relationships through position IDs is already established. E3 keeps IDs unique, preserves their multiset and role parity, and tests coherent versus conflicting relationships at inference. Success would demonstrate sensitivity to a supplied structure; it would not demonstrate spontaneous discovery of the new grouped serialization's semantics. [Primary paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.20671).

## What E3 can add, conditional on its results

The prospective contribution is a **controlled replication/extension candidate**, not an established first: unchanged checkpoints; all trained coordinates used once; key/value parity held fixed; grouped physical order and query location fixed; all slot permutations rather than a favorable swap; coherent controls matched to value-only maps; a specified inverse-permutation answer rule; six retained models; independent input audit; and published numeric predictions, gates and failures before outcomes. These are features of our proposed design, not claims that no earlier experiment used them.

A complete directed mapping with high alternate-answer accuracy would be stronger than demonstrating only accuracy loss or a logit shift, but its measured strength must be reported rather than assumed. It still would not distinguish an absolute coordinate-pairing table from a learned previous-coordinate relation, establish head-level mediation, or show that the model understands the grouped task under ordinary position assignment.

## Follow-ups that would materially sharpen the contribution

1. **Localize the causal stage.** In a separately registered follow-up, mediate the input-coordinate effect through L1 value-position states and L2 keys, while comparing query-path controls. Ask whether a minimal transferred pathway reproduces the exact inverse-permutation answer. This would distinguish an externally controllable behavior from a located binding computation and make comparison to Tang's path-specific intervention more direct.
2. **Compare formation with retrieval.** Contrast input-coordinate reassignment with late position/representation interventions after associations have formed. A dissociation could connect learned-APE coordinate dependence to the position-independent contextual binding tested by Feng and Steinhardt. Architecture/stage differences must be controlled rather than attributed to position scheme alone.
3. **Test the proposed rule outside the single grouped order.** Prospectively evaluate all or a fixed held-out set of pair-order-preserving physical serializations, keeping each key causally before its value and moving canonical IDs with tokens. This tests whether the rule survives changed causal exposure, rather than memorizing one rearrangement.
4. **Test a mechanistic boundary across training/position schemes.** Use independently trained models with absolute, relative/rotary, or no explicit positional encoding under a carefully matched task. Hold out complete seeds from mechanism selection and permit genuine failures. This would investigate when external coordinates determine binding rather than merely adding more successful examples of the same setting.

None of these are authorized retroactive changes to E3's primary grid. They require new prospective records and should follow an informative result or failure, not be searched until a preferred narrative appears.

## Search coverage and wording discipline

Searches included combinations of “position IDs,” “permutation,” “associative recall,” “binding,” “interchange,” “positional embeddings,” “swapping,” and “retrieval,” followed by original-paper inspection. This targeted search found close demonstrations; it did **not** establish whether every exact E3 design feature has or has not appeared together before. Some requested HTML versions were unavailable; alternative primary full texts were used where cited. No exhaustive code-level reproduction of prior work was attempted.

Safe prospective wording: “We test whether a complete, parity-preserving reassignment of trained positional coordinates predictably rebinds a toy transformer's answers, with coherent controls and predictions committed before evaluation.” Avoid “first,” “previously unknown,” “new binding mechanism,” or “AI discovered how transformers bind” unless substantially broader evidence later supports those claims. Novelty, predictive correctness and causal identifiability are separate questions.
