Which part knows?
Which part uses it?

Connecting AI parts is possible. The harder question is whether one part gives another part useful information—and why the connection works.

Recent papers sharpen our next experiment. First, lower the strength of our existing organ signal and measure what expert routing contributes. Later, test a message that must support a question the sending organ never sees.

Three changes to our research

  1. Test useful information. A correct message must help compared with no message. A wrong message must be measured separately.
  2. Separate accuracy from explanation. The same answer can survive a large change inside a model. That does not mean the internal computation stayed the same.
  3. Give each part a job we can check. First test prediction, communication and receiver use separately. Then connect them.

These choices follow the message controls in Draft-KV, the causal limits in Right Answer, Wrong Mechanism, and the complementary-parent direction in NinaXander. The specific experiment below is our adaptation.

Turn down the signal. Test the routes.

Our trained writer supplies an answer signal inside Granite, before its last four blocks. At full strength, all 96 answers stayed correct when we restored the expert choices and weights from the run without that signal. The routes still changed answer scores. We do not yet know whether the strong signal hid a larger routing effect on choices. See the completed result.

New fixed design / 96 existing cases

One signal. Three fixed strengths.

0No added signal
1/16One fixed attenuation
1Full saved signal

At each nonzero strength, compare the model’s current routes with the routes from zero strength. Compute the experts again on the current input. Also replay each condition’s own routes as a no-change control.

768 planned suffix evaluations. Each runs only the last four model blocks and output head. There is no new organ training, prompt search or paid model call.

Primary measurement: the target answer’s score minus the mean score of the seven other allowed answers. For each case and strength, subtract the fixed-old-route result from the current-route result. Compare this routing effect at full and reduced strength. Report correct choices, errors, ties and the lead over the strongest rival separately.

What would be informative? If restoring old routes hurts choices only at reduced strength, that supports a strength-dependent routing effect in this engineered channel. If it changes scores but not choices, routing affects the score without changing the measured answer. If neither strength shows an effect, the claim stays limited to those tested strengths.

Limits: all 96 cases have already been exposed. This tests the whole implemented routing policy, including expert choices, weights and numerical grouping effects. It does not isolate expert identities alone, prove a natural reasoning mechanism, or show an energy-model advantage. A three-point panel is not a full response curve. Signal size is not calibrated confidence.

Readiness: the fixed schedule and score helpers are prepared. The model collector and independent raw-output auditor still need implementation and admission checks. No new model results have been collected. Every no-change replay and saved endpoint must match exactly before interpretation. A failure remains a failure; the strength and cases will not be changed to rescue it.

Then test a message with more than one use

The bigger organ question needs a stronger design. Give the organ a private state with two independently changing facts. Build its message before the receiver gets a question. Reuse exactly the same message when the receiver is asked about either fact.

For example, two states can have the same parity label but different first and second bits. Swapping those states keeps the organ’s original task label unchanged while changing the correct answer to a later question. A channel restricted to that one label cannot solve the balanced component questions. A continuous message might retain both facts, so its allowed information must be explicit.

  1. Establish the receiver first. It must perform the selected operation when both facts are supplied normally. Our completed Granite on/off checks did not establish this. They stay closed.
  2. Compare interface-training recipes. Use an answer-trained connector, a connector trained to preserve receiver-useful state, and a receiver self-connection. Match capacity, examples and compute, and report differences in supervision.
  3. Remove and swap information. Compare no message, correct message, unrelated message and a state swap that preserves the donor’s original label. Keep the complete fixed state-by-question table.
  4. Confirm on untouched cases. Separate development, selection and confirmation. Freeze the message producer before confirmation. A reusable fact code would establish information reuse, not transfer of the donor’s algorithm.

Related composition tests already exist in Draft-KV and Arora and colleagues’ study. The possible contribution is an explanation of what information a training recipe preserves, loses, or fails to make usable. A two-bit demonstration alone would not be a publishable novelty claim.

Where energy and world models fit

After the communication test, use a small environment with exact rules. Compare an energy model, a conventional predictor and an exact simulator under the same information and compute budget. Use action alternatives that differ in a meaningful way, and keep the goal within the tested prediction horizon. This follows the directions and limitations in JEPA-Anything, The Planning Limits of Latent World Models and The Mouth is Not the Brain. It is a later design direction, not a registered or completed experiment.

What the recent papers actually say

A focused primary-source review, checked on 6 October 2026. This is not an exhaustive survey or a novelty guarantee. Most papers below are preprints. Each entry separates the paper’s evidence and open directions from our proposed change.

29 September 2026 · v1

NinaXander

Preprint · Sections IV–VII

What it found. A shared connector joins frozen language models from two different architecture families. No joined model matches the stronger parent. Reconstruction and transfer to another text domain remain weak.

Authors’ next steps or open limits. Choose parents with complementary strengths. Improve reconstruction, train across domains, test repeated switches and repeat across seeds.

Our change. Check that each part adds useful information. A working wire between models is already established prior work.

28 September 2026 · v1

Draft-KV

Preprint · Sections 5.3–6

What it found. In several earlier systems, unrelated messages barely changed accuracy. Draft-KV trains useful communication and also tests models with different private evidence.

Method lesson. The paper demonstrates matched-message, wrong-message and receiver-alone controls. It separates the benefit of correct messages from the damage caused by wrong ones.

Our change. Use both comparisons. A large correct-versus-wrong gap alone can hide a system that gains little from correct messages.

4 October 2026 · v2

Right Answer, Wrong Mechanism

Preprint · Sections 6–8; Appendix B

What it found. An internal patch can get the right answer through a planted alternative pathway. The revision tests models up to 7B parameters. Its tests do not establish how common this is in natural models.

Authors’ next steps or open limits. Detecting alternative pathways whose individual units stay within normal ranges remains open. Broader downstream checks and stricter natural reference sets are needed.

Our change. Call our next result an effect of a controlled intervention. Correct answers and normal-looking signal sizes cannot prove that the native reasoning process was recovered.

1 October 2026 · v1

Are We Recovering Mechanisms?

Preprint · Sections 6

What it found. Scores used to judge recovered circuits can disagree with an independently fixed behavior test. Output agreement alone does not identify a unique mechanism.

Authors’ next steps or open limits. Report behavioral deficits alongside circuit scores. Compare multiple measures and interventions, and control circuit size and intervention choices.

Our change. Report answer choices, ties and score changes separately. Keep all cases. Do not select a favorable score after seeing the result.

17 September 2026 · v1

JEPA-Anything

Preprint · Sections 3.3.4; 4–6

What it found. The model combines predictive factors across several data types. Geometric separation of these factors does not guarantee separate meanings or causes. Gains do not occur in every control environment.

Authors’ next steps or open limits. Use predictive components in systems that choose interventions, compare expected and observed outcomes, and use errors to guide the next experiment.

Our change. For a later world-model organ, use a small environment with known rules and meaningful action changes. Compare a learned energy scorer with an ordinary predictor and an exact simulator.

30 September 2026 · v1

The Planning Limits of Latent World Models

Preprint · Sections 7; Appendices F and P

What it found. Planning can fail when a goal is much farther away than the model can reliably predict. Even an exact simulator suffers under a short planning horizon. Easy random action comparisons can hide differences.

Authors’ next steps or open limits. Generate useful subgoals without access to an expert rollout. Balance prediction horizon and search budget. Test harder nearby action alternatives; many comparisons still need more seeds.

Our change. Match the prediction and goal horizons. Separate predictor errors from planner errors with an exact-model control. Harder questions must isolate a cause, rather than simply increase difficulty.

11 June 2026 · v3

V-JEPA 2.1

Preprint · Sections 5

What it found. Dense predictive training improves visual representations. Better representations are a starting point for building a world model; they are not by themselves evidence of a useful language-model organ.

Authors’ next steps or open limits. Scale training and use the new features to build world models, with fine-grained robotics and navigation as directions.

Our change. Test action-conditioned predictions and their use by a receiver separately. We do not need to train a large video model to test the interface question.

28 August 2026 · v2

Launch-Bound and Substitutable

Preprint · Sections IV–V

What it found. In the tested OLMoE quantization setting, restoring original routes explains little of the quality loss. Changing fewer routes is not a reliable quality measure. The causal replay result is model-specific.

Authors’ next steps or open limits. Remove a router-quantization confound in DeepSeek, test expert batching, and judge routing interventions by output quality rather than route-change counts alone.

Our change. Measure routing effects at fixed input signals. Our proposed signal-strength comparison is our hypothesis, not an experiment requested by these authors.

28 September 2026 · v1

Causal and Interpretable Structures in LLM Compositional Tasks

Preprint · Sections 4.2; 5–7

What it found. The authors separate an intermediate relation from a final answer and transplant that relation while preserving the recipient context. An orderly representation can still be causally unused.

Authors’ next steps or open limits. Extend beyond cyclic tasks with three variables, examine mixed concepts and investigate neurons. Sampling becomes expensive as the number of variables grows.

Our change. Intermediate transfer already has direct precedent. A private-information test must explain a specific failure or success beyond demonstrating another connection.

2 June 2026 · v3

Revisiting Model Stitching in the Foundation Model Era

Accepted at CVPR 2026; arXiv version checked · Sections 4.1; 5.2–5.4; 6.2

What it found. In vision models, training a connector to match receiver features can help. Connecting the receiver to itself is an important comparison. A weaker source does not always help a stronger target.

Authors’ next steps or open limits. Develop task-agnostic hybrid vision backbones and extend the resource-conscious stitch-tree design.

Our change. Compare interface-training recipes with matched capacity, examples and compute. Account for different supervision, and include a receiver self-connection. Vision results motivate this test but do not establish language transfer.

31 March 2026 · v2

The Mouth is Not the Brain

ICLR 2026 World Models workshop paper, per arXiv metadata · Sections 7.2–7.3

What it found. An energy-based model connects to frozen GPT-2 through a trained adapter to generate product reviews. This models structured attributes, rather than changes through time.

Authors’ next steps or open limits. Test time-series data, more stable alternatives such as variational autoencoders, better evaluation, and other domains and languages.

Our change. Energy-plus-language is already prior work. To claim an energy-model benefit, compare matched alternatives on prediction and downstream use. Our GPT-2 work remains paused.

23 August 2026 · V1

Cross-Model Stitching Transfers Answers, Not Computation

Preprint · Sections 6–7; 9

What it found. Task-trained linear connectors in the tested same-family language models mainly transfer first-answer information. This is not a theorem that all model composition must fail.

Authors’ next steps or open limits. Cross-family transfer and whether other stitches can transmit missing computation remain open.

Our change. Hide the later question from the sending organ and connector. A reusable state message could then answer several questions, but this would still show information transfer, not transfer of an algorithm.
