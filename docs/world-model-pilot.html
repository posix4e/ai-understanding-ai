World-model pilot / 6 October 2026

The door mattered more than the average score.

A model can predict most moves correctly and still miss the one move needed to reach many goals.

We trained two kinds of small world model, then asked each to guide a planner through a key-and-door world. The energy models predicted more unseen moves correctly. That did not give them consistently better navigation.

Six local training runs · Three seeds per model · Independent numerical checks passed · $0 provider spending · One-map development result

The clearest result: all three energy models solved 391 of 600 navigation tasks with 16-step look-ahead. After we replaced one door-entry prediction with the exact world rule, each solved all 600.

This repair was chosen after we saw the pilot results. It uses the correct answer from the simulator. It shows that this transition is sufficient to remove the failures in this setup; it is not a learned repair or evidence of a general energy-model advantage.

The small world

The wall splits a 5 × 5 grid. The agent must collect a key before it can enter the door. A state records both position and key possession. From the fixed initial state, 30 states are reachable. Four actions give 120 possible transitions.

Both models saw the same 96 training transitions. We held out 24, including one key-pickup and one door-entry example. All six runs learned the 96 training transitions correctly. We evaluated every one of the 600 reachable start–goal pairs, including failures.

The energy models got 19–22 of 24 unseen transitions right; the ordinary predictors got 10–11. Yet one ordinary-model seed navigated all 600 tasks at horizon 16, while the other two reached 380. This variation prevents a broad model-ranking claim. Planning uses the full probability distribution and observes the true state after each action; it is not a sequence of guesses made without feedback.

Even exact world rules gave only 365 of 600 successes with one-step look-ahead, compared with 600 at16 steps. This isolates a limitation of this planner, heuristic, tie rule and map. It does not mean every goal beyond a planner’s horizon is impossible.

What the repair tells us

All three energy models incorrectly ranked “stay here” first for the held-out move that enters the door from the left while holding the key. We replaced that whole predicted distribution with the exact next state and left every other distribution unchanged.

The navigation total rose from 391 to 600 in each energy run. Correcting the held-out key-pickup prediction alone left each total at 391. The ordinary predictors rose from600,380,380 to 600,577,566 after the door repair. Replacing all 24 held-out transition distributions gave600 for all six models.

These controlled changes test sensitivity to specific predictions. They do not show that one scalar probability or one hidden feature is the unique cause. A wrong transition can change the planner’s preferred route far away from that transition.

The next test is complete

We tested four observations on new fixed maps. On eight reserved evaluation maps, random choice gave 54.62% navigation success and decision sensitivity gave 51.54%. We also tested why the ordinary predictor above succeeded despite its errors: full probability distributions and true-state feedback both mattered under the fixed diagnostic changes. Read all results, limits and checked evidence. The original pilot and exact-rule repairs reported on this page remain unchanged.

This follows our review of predictive models that choose interventions and planning-horizon controls. A useful world-model organ must predict consequences and help a receiving system act. We have started with the prediction and planning parts.

Limits and evidence

  • This is a fully observed symbolic world. It is not a visual JEPA model, a language-model splice or a new architecture.
  • The energy scorer and ordinary predictor use the same categorical likelihood objective and nearly equal parameter counts. They do not use equal compute.
  • The valid-state vocabulary is supplied to both. We did not test new maps or independent confirmation data.
  • The repair test was designed after seeing the pilot, uses ground truth, and performs no new training.
  • Independent code reconstructed the world, scores, planner decisions and repairs. It did not repeat training gradients. All earlier studies and STOP records remain closed.
